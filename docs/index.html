01 — System Overview

Five Technical Phases,
One Composited Frame

The project establishes a complete Virtual Production pipeline in which two physically co-located cameras — a Sony α mirrorless and a ZED 2i depth camera — are first synchronized in time, then calibrated in space. This results a uni-sensor system where the ZED 2i acts as the driving source of

  • SLAM-based Base-free Tracking
  • Depth sensor for real-time Occlusion Mapping
  • a 38-joint skeleton for MoCap and embodied interaction.

    • The result is a full XR system with unified sensory input, providing a base-free, suite-free, controller-free system.
    The system combines Virtual production, and Augmented Reality to create a full immersive system, with an embodied LMA-based interactive layer. With that, the system operates in two different modes, a compositing mode, and an interactive mode.

    01 / TEMPORAL

    Camera Sync

    Each camera's own frame timestamps, a shared cover event and the correlation of image motion give the time offset between the two independently clocked cameras. Every Sony frame is then paired with the ZED frame taken at the same moment.

    02 / SPATIAL

    Stereo Calibration

    ChArUco-board calibration measures the Sony lens and the rotation and translation between the two cameras, tested on held-out views and against the ZED's own depth. It places the virtual camera and the live depth in the Sony's exact viewpoint.

    03 / OCCLUSION

    Depth-Correct Compositing

    The ZED depth is cleaned on the Jetson, streamed to Unreal Engine and reprojected into the Sony's view, so that real people and objects hide CG elements behind them, pixel by pixel, with a moving camera.

    04 / KINEMATIC

    Skeleton Denoising

    ZED BODY_38 yields 38-joint 3D coordinates at 30 Hz. The 1 Euro Filter suppresses geometric sensor noise per-joint before any LMA descriptor is computed from the stream.

    05 / EMBODIED

    Kinematic AR

    Four continuous LMA Effort scalars computed from the denoised skeleton data. The cleaned skeletal data drives the Niagara particle systems and UE5 material WPO deformations. Movement quality — not position — modulates the interactive virtual particles.

    02 — The Pipeline

    From Raw Footage
    to Live AR Performance

    The system consists of four main technical phases. Each phase has an offline, Python-based, containerized implementation, followed by a C++ and HLSL real-time implementation and Blueprint configuration inside Unreal Engine 5. The offline side measures and validates; the runtime side uses the measured values every frame.

    03 — Camera Alignment

    Two Cameras,
    One Reference Frame

    The Sony α and the ZED 2i are mounted on the same rig but operate as fully independent devices with different sensors, different lenses and different clocks. Before a ZED depth value can be used in the Sony image, two questions must be answered: when did both cameras see the same moment, and where exactly is the Sony relative to the ZED? The figures below come from one calibration recording (dataset_05, about five minutes).

    Temporal Alignment

    The ZED stores a timestamp with every frame; the Sony recording has an exact, constant 30 frames per second. Both clocks run in real time, so the two time axes differ only by an offset. It is measured in two independent ways that must agree: the moment both lenses are covered with a card (visible as a dark period in both cameras), and the correlation of image motion between the two cameras over thousands of frames. The pipeline then checks that the offset does not drift over the recording and pairs every Sony frame with the ZED frame taken closest in time. Frames the ZED dropped are excluded rather than guessed.

    Spatial Calibration

    A ChArUco board (11 × 8 squares, DICT_4X4_50) is held still in many poses in front of both cameras. Because the board's size is known, every view gives corners whose 3D positions are known and whose image positions are measured. The pipeline is built so that errors cannot hide inside each other: the ZED's lens values come from its SDK, the Sony's lens is measured from Sony views alone, and the rotation and translation between the cameras are then solved with both lenses fixed. Every result is tested on views it was not fitted on.

    Validation — Does the Calibration Predict?

    The validation does not ask how well the calibration fits its own data, but how well it predicts new data. The board pose seen by the ZED is moved into the Sony with the calibration and compared with where the Sony actually saw the corners (corner transfer, 2.00 px on held-out pairs). The same test through the ZED's measured depth, the path the live system uses, gives 1.53 px. A "Sony floor" (1.29 px: the board pose fitted to the Sony alone) shows what no calibration can remove. The calibration is graded on what it adds above that floor: 1.53 px, Acceptable.

    04 — Depth Occlusion

    Real Surfaces
    in Front of CG

    For every pixel of the Sony image, the composite decides whether the real scene or the CG element is closer to the camera. The real distance comes from the ZED depth map; the CG distance from Unreal Engine. When the real surface is closer, the camera image wins and the performer hides the CG. Depth errors show up in two ways: CG appearing on the performer (show-through), and holes cut into CG around the performer, where unreliable pixels claim to be near. Both concentrate along silhouettes, where stereo matching cannot tell foreground from background.

    Gate · Jetson
    Pixels whose ZED confidence value is above 50 are removed and sent as an explicit "invalid" code, separate from "far".
    Stream · NDI
    Depth encoded as 8-bit codes: 0–235 for 0.5–6.0 m, 255 for invalid, with a guard band against compression errors.
    Reproject · HLSL
    For every Sony pixel, a walk along its line of sight through the ZED depth map finds the first real surface, using the calibration above. Invalid pixels are stepped over; areas hidden from the ZED take the background depth behind them.
    Median · 5×5
    Removes isolated depth speckles without moving edges.
    Matte
    Depth comparison with a small bias towards CG, a 1-pixel erosion of the real-world cut-out and a temporal average.
    Edges
    Feathering and image-guided snapping of the matte edge to edges in the Sony image.
    A depth map is not a picture. Unreal Engine treats an NDI video texture as an sRGB colour image and converts every value from sRGB to linear light when it is read. For depth codes this compressed all distances towards the camera: a performer at 2.0 m was read at about 0.8 m, and the reprojection over-corrected the parallax between the two cameras, placing the silhouette up to 130 px too high at close range. Converting the values back in the shader put the depth silhouette on the performer and removed most of the edge artefacts the later filters had been compensating for.
    Less filtering after the fix. A hole-fill stage that copied the nearest valid depth into invalid pixels was removed: once the reprojection stepped over invalid pixels correctly, the fill only enlarged the performer's cut-out and made its edges flicker. Each remaining stage was re-tuned on its own, in pipeline order, rather than tuned together on the final image.

    05 — Kinematic Extraction

    Denoising
    the Skeleton Stream

    The ZED SDK's HUMAN_BODY_ACCURATE model tracks 38 joints in the BODY_38 format at 30 Hz, outputting 3D world-space coordinates in millimetres for every joint in every frame where the performer's root (pelvis) is confidently located. Raw joint positions carry sensor noise that corrupt any downstream descriptor computation. The 1 Euro Filter resolves this.

    LMA Expansiveness — The First Metric

    Before the descriptor system runs in real time, the Expansiveness metric is computed offline as an analytical validation of the denoised skeleton stream. It measures the maximum reach distance from the midspine (spine_2) to either wrist, the most direct proxy for the performer's use of kinesphere space in Laban's sense. The signal peaks correspond to large open gestures; valleys mark arms held close to the body.

    LMA Kinesphere Expansiveness max(spine_2 → left_wrist, spine_2 → right_wrist)
    Y-axis: Euclidean distance from spine_2 to the farther wrist, in mm. Computed from raw (unfiltered) positions to preserve full dynamic range. This time-series is the foundational validation for the Expansiveness descriptor used in the live system.
    06 — Embodied Interaction

    Four Qualities - Real-Time Interaction

    The system does not classify gesture types or respond only to body position. It computes four continuous scalar descriptors from the live denoised ZED skeletal stream on every tick, each corresponding to one of Laban's four Effort factors. The eight canonical LMA Action Drives emerge as regions in the resulting four-dimensional quality space rather than being explicitly detected.

    Each descriptor output passes through a calibrated one Euro filter which serves as an adaptive smoothing and gating mechanism. The filter's cutoff frequency is tuned per-descriptor to create a dead zone around the neutral point of each quality dimension, producing no AR response, and a saturation threshold above which the response plateaus. This ensures the virtual environment responds only to intentional expressive movement, not postural micro-adjustments or sensor residuals.

    LMA Time — Sudden ↔ Sustained

    Effort

    Weighted RMS velocity of key limb joints — wrists, elbows, shoulders, spine, head. Measures the energetic urgency of movement. The same physical position produces any Effort value depending on how the body arrived there.

    → Niagara · particle emission rate & velocity
    LMA Space — Expanded ↔ Contracted

    Expansiveness

    The distance from the wrists and elbows to spine_2. The distance of each of those limbs to the spine captures kinesphere reach correctly. The spatial gesture defines the body's spatial attitude. Force direction follows the limb-to-spine vector.

    → MPC · WPO deformation frequency · RadialForce radius
    LMA Weight — Strong ↔ Light

    Weight

    Acceleration magnitude across key joints — the rate of velocity change. Independent of Effort: a body can move fast with low acceleration (quick drift) or fast with high acceleration (punch). High Weight means force is being exerted or absorbed.

    → MPC · WPO surface bend amplitude
    LMA Flow — Free ↔ Bound

    Flow

    The variability of movement over time. High flow — erratic, unpredictable — reads as Free. Low flow — changes predictably — reads as Bound.

    → Niagara · attractor strength  ·  MPC · noise scale

    The Eight Action Drives

    07 — Demonstration

    A Grid That Listens
    to How You Move

    Live AR Grid — Conceptual
    Float

    The demonstration places the performer at the centre of a three-dimensional grid of physics-simulated cubic objects.

    Cubes in front of the performer are occluded by the depth comparison pipeline — the body correctly appears in front of them. Cubes behind are hidden behind the body. A camera rotating around the performer continuously renegotiates these depth relationships in three dimensions.

    The grid responds to movement quality. An arm thrust forward with high force pushes the grid forward along the arm's direction vector. A slow open expansion spreads the grid outward. A sudden explosive movement scatters the cubes and floods the space with particles.

    The system reads the body's expressive state, not only its location. A stationary performer exerting high muscular tension produces a different grid response than the same position held without tension.
